OnRecord Studio

A macOS workspace for people who live inside public records requests. It turns a pile of portal responses into a corpus you can query, audit, and hand to someone else with the evidence attached.

Everything runs on your Mac. No account, no server, no analytics service. Your corpus is a local database file that never leaves the machine.

Sold on the Mac App Store as a one-time purchase. No subscription, and no license key to enter.

Collect

  • Ingest from public records portals, from local files, or from pasted content.
  • One rate limiter per host, so a multi-year sweep stays polite to every agency it touches.
  • An interrupted run resumes from a durable work frontier instead of starting over.
  • Redirects are returned to the caller and re-validated, never followed blindly.
  • The client identifies itself as OnRecord-Studio/1.0 public-records-audit-client. It does not impersonate a browser.

Read what they actually send

PDF, spreadsheets, Outlook .msg, archives, RTF, HTML, Office packages, and plain text, with a salvage path for damaged files. Nested containers unpack under depth and size bounds. Databases you supply open strictly read-only.

Analyze

  • Timeline anomalies across a request's recorded lifecycle.
  • Departmental outlier ranking by z-score against the rest of your corpus.
  • Redaction density over extracted text.
  • Keyword clustering and density maps, computed locally.
  • An arbitrary-closure index for requests closed unusually fast.

California statutory evaluation

Deadline evaluation implements the California Public Records Act: the ten-day determination window, the fourteen-day extension, and the twenty-four-day extended maximum, cited to Government Code section 7922.535. That is the one statute the app counts against. Everything else it reports is a comparison against peers, and it is labeled that way. A slower-than-average agency is not a violation, and the app will not call it one.

Evidence integrity

  • C2PA manifests read live from the asset, with an explicit confidence ladder.
  • Redaction integrity checks and a residual-evidence audit, for when a released document still carries recoverable content underneath.
  • Document version history carved from the file where the format preserves it.
  • SHA-256 digests recorded for raw files and for extracted text.
  • A verification log recording what was checked, when, and against what.

Export something defensible

Produce a brief with its source documents, the legal authorities you imported, the findings, and a manifest, signed with Ed25519 so a recipient can tell whether the package changed after you made it. Personally identifying text is masked on the way out.

Requirements

  • macOS 13 Ventura or later.
  • A universal build: native on Apple silicon and on Intel.
  • Runs in the macOS App Sandbox. It reads the files and folders you hand it, and nothing else.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Statutory deadline counting covers California only. Collection, parsing, and evidence handling are region-neutral, but no other state or federal statute is implemented.
  • Search requires every term you type. There is no stemming, relevance ranking, phrase matching, or boolean operators.
  • An absence is displayed as an absence, never as a zero. A field the app did not measure does not get a default.
  • It is analysis tooling. It does not give legal advice and does not decide whether an agency broke the law.
