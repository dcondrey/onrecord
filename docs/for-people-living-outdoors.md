# For people living outdoors: you deserve respect and a way back to your request

You do not have to explain your whole life to deserve help. On Record is intended to let an advocate
write down what you are asking for, read it back to you, record your consent, and give you a paper
recovery card that works from a borrowed phone, kiosk, or another partner site.

The public map should not contain your full name, date of birth, ZIP, health information, where you
sleep, or anything that could identify or endanger you. Ask what will be public before you agree. You
can decline optional information, ask for a correction, withdraw consent where applicable, and ask an
advocate what happens if you lose the card.

The card is not proof of who you are. It is a way to return to your own request. Keep it private. If it
is lost, ask an advocate for an in-person restore rather than giving the code to someone who promises
help. Providers should not be able to mark their own promise complete; you get to say whether an answer
actually happened.

People may be frustrated by tents, noise, trash, or blocked paths. That frustration does not erase your
rights or dignity. You can also help protect yourself and your neighbors by choosing safer locations,
keeping exits and accessible paths clear, reducing noise during quiet hours, and asking where toilets,
showers, drinking water, trash disposal, sharps disposal, charging, outreach, and storage are available.
Rules should be explained, applied consistently, and never used as an excuse for harassment or violence.

If you are in immediate danger, seek emergency help. If a site, outreach worker, or member of the public
threatens you, tell a trusted advocate and ask how to document it safely. You should never have to trade
your privacy or safety for basic information.
