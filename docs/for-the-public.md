# For the public: seeing people, not stereotypes

People living outside are neighbors. A tent, a backpack, a tired face, or a request for a bathroom is
not a character judgment. People lose housing for many reasons: rent increases, illness, violence,
job loss, family separation, disability, discrimination, or systems that are hard to navigate. A short
interaction rarely tells you the whole story.

It is understandable to feel frustrated when a sidewalk is blocked, trash is left behind, noise keeps
you awake, or you are unsure how to respond. Frustration is real. It does not make cruelty, harassment,
filming people without permission, taking belongings, or treating everyone as dangerous acceptable.

If you want to help, ask before approaching, keep a respectful distance, offer a specific practical
choice, and accept “no.” Share service information without demanding a story. Do not photograph, name,
or post a person’s location. For urgent danger, call emergency services; for a non-emergency welfare
concern, use the local outreach or coordinated-entry route rather than confronting someone.

On Record is designed to make requests and responses more visible without turning a person into public
content. Read the privacy notice, do not add protected information, and remember that a public map is
not a shelter-placement feed. Compassion and boundaries can coexist: keep shared spaces usable while
recognizing the dignity and agency of the people who need them.
