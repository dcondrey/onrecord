# Know Your Rights: a practical California and San Diego guide

**Educational information, not legal advice.** Laws, court orders, local rules, and enforcement practices
change. This guide is written for general orientation, not to predict what will happen in a particular
encounter. If you are in immediate danger, prioritize safety and call emergency services. For legal
help, contact a qualified attorney or legal-aid organization.

## Interacting with law enforcement

If you are stopped, stay as safe as you can. Keep your hands visible, do not run, lie, physically resist,
or reach unexpectedly. You can ask: **“Am I free to go?”** If yes, leave calmly. If no, ask why you are
being detained. You generally have the right to remain silent; say clearly, **“I am exercising my right
to remain silent.”** If arrested, ask for a lawyer and avoid discussing the facts of the case until you
have legal advice. Do not consent to a search; say, **“I do not consent to a search,”** but never
physically resist. Different rules can apply when driving, at borders, on probation, or under a search
condition.

You may record officers or sanitation workers performing their duties in public when doing so does not
interfere or put you in danger. Do not post a person’s face, camp, medical information, or location
without consent. ACLU SoCal’s current guide explains these rights and cautions in more detail:
[When stopped by an officer](https://www.aclusocal.org/know-your-rights/when-stopped-officer/).

## Encampment sweeps, citations, and property seizures

San Diego’s rules are location- and circumstance-specific. The City’s current Unsafe Camping Ordinance
page describes progressive enforcement, service offers, and locations where enforcement may apply even
when shelter availability differs. Read the current [City ordinance overview](https://www.sandiego.gov/police/services/neighborhood-policing-division/unsafe-camping)
and the [abatement procedure](https://www.sandiego.gov/homelessness-strategies-and-solutions/services/abatement)
rather than relying on an old flyer or rumor.

If a cleanup is announced:

1. Photograph or write down the notice, date, time, location, agency, and contact information when safe.
2. Ask what property can be removed, what will be stored, where it will be stored, and how to retrieve it.
3. Move identification, medication, paperwork, mobility devices, phones, and irreplaceable items first
   if you can do so safely. Ask for an accommodation if a disability makes moving difficult.
4. Request an inventory or receipt for property taken. Keep the notice and retrieval information.
5. Do not physically fight a crew or officer. Ask an advocate or legal-aid provider to help challenge a
   missing, damaged, or discarded item.

The City describes advance notice, storage of items of value or apparent utility, and retrieval notices,
but real-world application can be disputed. A citation is not proof that you are guilty. Do not ignore
the court date or instructions on the citation; ask legal aid what signing and responding mean in your
case. A citation, arrest, property loss, or unsafe conduct should be documented after you are safe.

## Denial of public services and discrimination

People experiencing homelessness still have rights to equal treatment and reasonable disability
accommodations. A provider or public program may have eligibility rules, capacity limits, safety rules,
or documentation requirements, but it should explain them consistently and provide an accessible way to
ask questions or appeal. Discrimination based on a protected characteristic, retaliation for asserting
rights, denial of language access, or refusal to provide a reasonable accommodation may be reportable.

Write down the date, place, staff names, exact words, witnesses, requested accommodation, documents, and
what happened next. Ask for the policy in writing. California’s Civil Rights Department accepts
information and complaints about discrimination in public services and accommodations; its contact
center is listed at [calcivilrights.ca.gov/contactus](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/contactus/).
For county benefits and services, see the [CDSS civil-rights complaint information](https://www.cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/reporting/file-a-complaint/discrimination-complaints).
LawHelpCA can help locate legal resources and translated materials: [lawhelpca.org](https://www.lawhelpca.org/).

## What to do if your rights may have been violated

- Get to safety and medical care first. If injured, ask a clinician to document injuries.
- Write a timeline while memory is fresh: agency, names/badge numbers, witnesses, exact words, locations,
  property, notices, citations, and any video or photographs.
- Preserve originals. Do not publish sensitive evidence publicly; keep a copy with a trusted advocate or
  attorney. Redact other people’s faces, names, medical details, and camp locations before sharing.
- Ask the relevant agency for its complaint process. A complaint is separate from a lawsuit and does not
  guarantee an investigation or result.
- Contact legal aid, ACLU SoCal, Disability Rights California, or a local attorney. Ask about deadlines;
  some claims have short notice or filing periods.
- Do not use this app to publish an accusation or identifying information. On Record can help an advocate
  document a consented request for legal or service support, but it is not a legal intake system.
