# Operating model: support before scorekeeping

On Record should complement—not duplicate—HMIS, coordinated entry, Shelter Ready, or a provider’s
case-management system.

## Safe public interaction

- People do not post directly to a public person-level channel. Intake is advocate-mediated, consented,
  de-identified, and reviewed before publication.
- The public can make a structured offer (item, fee, or service), but cannot comment directly on a
  person’s record. Offers are queued for advocate review and routing; names and contact details are
  not requested.
- Moderation should use rate limits, abuse reporting, content filtering, a community code of conduct,
  and a documented escalation path. Never expose a requester to a hostile comment thread.

## Provider accountability without punishment

The dashboard reports documented response gaps and confirmed outcomes. It is not a provider score,
ranking, or public shame list. A gap may reflect capacity, eligibility, closures, staffing, or a
missing integration—not unwillingness. Add context, allow provider correction, and publish positive
response badges only when the underlying event is verified and consented.

Providers should be able to acknowledge, defer with a date, or decline with a reason. The requester
still controls whether a provider claim becomes an answered outcome. No provider can resolve its own
claim unilaterally.

## Integration over duplicate data entry

Do not ask shelters to maintain a second public bed inventory. Use sourced capacity references and
intake instructions in the public map. If an authorized partner later supplies operational data, keep
it behind role-based access, show freshness and provenance, and never present it as a public placement
guarantee. Mutual-aid offers and localized grassroots responses are the natural first-class use cases
for this layer; HMIS remains the system of record for protected client and program data.
