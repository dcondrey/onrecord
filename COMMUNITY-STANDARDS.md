# Community standards for On Record

## Why these rules matter

There is a person on the other side of every request—not a debate prompt, not a target, and not a
backdrop for someone else’s anger. Many people who use this project have already had their names,
belongings, bodies, or stories handled without permission. A careless joke, a flood of spam, or a guessed
location can create real danger long after the comment disappears.

The easiest test is simple: **would you say this to the person, face to face, while remembering that they
may be tired, frightened, or unable to answer?** If not, rewrite it. If you are upset, pause. The few
seconds you spend choosing dignity may be the difference between someone asking for help again and someone
deciding that every system is unsafe.

These standards apply to GitHub Discussions, issues, pull requests, the On Record website when it becomes
interactive, moderation tools, and official project spaces.

## Welcome here

- Questions asked in good faith, including basic questions.
- Lived experience shared voluntarily and without pressure to identify yourself.
- Respectful disagreement focused on ideas, evidence, safety, and tradeoffs.
- Plain-language corrections, translations, accessibility feedback, and constructive criticism.
- Offers of practical help that do not demand a story, payment, personal data, or public gratitude.
- Reports of harm, privacy risks, discrimination, bugs, and unintended consequences.

## Not acceptable

- Spam, repeated promotion, referral abuse, bot floods, or messages unrelated to the conversation.
- Hateful, dehumanizing, racist, sexist, homophobic, transphobic, ableist, xenophobic, classist, or
  otherwise discriminatory content.
- Threats, harassment, stalking, intimidation, sexual attention, humiliation, or coordinated pile-ons.
- Doxxing or guessing/publishing a person’s name, face, health details, recovery code, immigration
  information, phone number, workplace, shelter, encampment, or precise location.
- Sharing private messages, screenshots, records, or identifying “case studies” without informed consent.
- Impersonation, fraud, coercive fundraising, predatory offers, malware, phishing, or requests for secrets.
- Posting someone’s story to win an argument, score political points, attract attention, or invite strangers
  to investigate them.
- Telling a person to “just move,” “prove” their hardship, disclose trauma, or accept unsafe conditions
  as the price of respect.
- Encouraging illegal harm, vigilantism, retaliation, or interference with emergency/service operations.
- Using AI or automation to manufacture testimonials, astroturf agreement, target a person, or overwhelm
  moderation.

## When a conversation gets heated

Stop and ask what outcome would help the person most. Address the claim, not the character. Replace
accusations with a question; replace a demand for private details with a request for a safe, public fact.
If you made a mistake, edit or retract it, say what changed, and do not make the harmed person teach you
in public. A moderator may pause comments when continuing would create more risk than understanding.

## Moderation and enforcement

Moderators may remove content, limit posting, close a thread, require edits, or suspend accounts. They may
act immediately for threats, doxxing, hateful abuse, scams, or privacy exposure. For less urgent issues,
the usual sequence is explain → invite correction → remove or restrict if it continues. A moderation
decision may be appealed privately; appeals must not repeat the harmful material.

Report conduct to the project’s published conduct contact. Do not put a real person’s protected details
into a public report. If someone is in immediate danger, use emergency services rather than waiting for a
moderator.

## A promise to the people this project serves

We will not confuse visibility with help. We will protect a person’s ability to say no, correct the record,
withdraw, and return safely. We will remember that “winning” a discussion is never worth making a person
less safe asking for help.
