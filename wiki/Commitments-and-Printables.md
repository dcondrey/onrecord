# Commitments and printables

These materials are voluntary tools for communication and self-governance. None is a lease, tenancy,
private-property authorization, legal defense, safety guarantee, or protection from enforcement.

## Shared community commitments

Everyone has responsibilities, and responsibilities must be matched with dignity, safety, and realistic
options. These commitments are not a substitute for law, an emergency response, a shelter bed, or a
provider’s legal obligations. They are a starting point for reducing harm and making concerns easier to
resolve without humiliation or escalation.

### People living outdoors

When it is safe and possible, a person living outdoors may commit to:

- keep sidewalks, curb ramps, doorways, fire lanes, transit access, and emergency routes clear;
- bag and dispose of waste, never burn trash, and place sharps in a rigid closed container for safe
  disposal;
- keep cooking, heat, batteries, generators, pets, visitors, and belongings within a safe footprint;
- reduce noise during agreed quiet hours and avoid threats, harassment, violence, or property damage;
- protect neighbors’ privacy and never publish another person’s name, health details, recovery code, or
  exact sleeping location;
- ask for help early when a conflict, medical issue, fire risk, or unsafe condition is developing; and
- leave or relocate when a voluntary permission agreement ends, where doing so is safe and lawful.

These commitments do not require someone to surrender belongings, accept harassment, disclose medical or
immigration information, or move into danger. A person may ask for an accommodation or an advocate.

### Local businesses and property representatives

When choosing to welcome or interact with people living outdoors, a business or property representative
may commit to:

- identify the authorized decision-maker and state boundaries, hours, facilities, and conditions plainly;
- use the voluntary [private-property permission form](https://github.com/writerslogic/onrecord/blob/main/docs/private-property-camping-permission.md)
  when appropriate, retaining copies for both sides;
- keep public access, fire exits, ADA routes, utilities, and customer safety clear without humiliating
  or profiling people;
- offer practical information about bathrooms, water, trash, outreach, and emergency contacts when
  available, without demanding unnecessary personal data;
- give reasonable notice before ending a voluntary arrangement when safety allows, and explain where
  belongings may be retrieved;
- never retaliate against someone for requesting an accommodation, reporting harm, or declining an
  optional disclosure; and
- call emergency services for immediate danger, not as a default substitute for respectful communication
  or outreach.

This is not a promise that a business must permit camping or that a signed form overrides law, zoning,
fire, health, insurance, landlord, or accessibility requirements.

### Service providers and outreach teams

Providers may commit to:

- explain eligibility, wait times, alternatives, privacy, and limits in plain language;
- collect the minimum information necessary and never publish names, precise locations, recovery
  material, health details, or victim-service information;
- use individual accounts, role-limited access, confidentiality agreements, MFA, encryption, and
  auditable records for protected work;
- record neutral events such as acknowledged, referred, scheduled, completed, declined, or withdrawn,
  with context rather than punitive scores;
- let the requester correct, withdraw, appeal, or ask for an advocate, interpreter, accommodation, or
  paper alternative;
- avoid promising a bed, placement, benefit, or outcome that is not confirmed; and
- coordinate with HMIS/coordinated entry only through approved data-sharing agreements and protected
  workflows.

Providers should not mark a requester’s need complete without a requester or advocate confirmation.

### Government officials and law enforcement

Officials and officers may commit to:

- communicate rules, notices, citations, property procedures, and appeal routes clearly and accessibly;
- apply policies consistently without profiling, retaliation, or punishment for poverty, disability, or
  requesting help;
- prioritize safety, de-escalation, outreach, and reasonable accommodations where policy and law permit;
- protect medication, identification, mobility devices, documents, and essential belongings during any
  authorized operation, with inventory and retrieval information where required;
- distinguish a voluntary community pledge from legal authorization, and never treat a QR sticker as a
  waiver of rights;
- provide supervisor, complaint, records, and legal-aid contact information; and
- document actions accurately and preserve relevant records according to law and policy.

This section does not state what a particular officer or agency is legally required to do. See [Know Your
Rights](Know-Your-Rights) and current official guidance for the applicable jurisdiction.

### General public and neighbors

Members of the public may commit to:

- treat people living outdoors as neighbors, not spectacles or threats by default;
- ask before approaching, keep a respectful distance, accept “no,” and avoid filming, doxxing, or taking
  belongings;
- report urgent danger through emergency services and non-emergency concerns through outreach or an
  appropriate local channel rather than confrontation;
- keep shared paths usable without using insults, threats, or vigilante enforcement;
- offer specific practical help—water, a service referral, or a mutual-aid offer—without demanding a
  story or personal information; and
- avoid posting a person’s face, tent, medical information, recovery material, or exact location.

Compassion and boundaries can coexist. A clean-up concern can be raised without denying someone’s dignity.

## Respectful Neighbor Commitment

The occupant voluntarily commits to bag and dispose of waste, keep sidewalks/curb ramps/doorways/fire
lanes clear, reduce noise during agreed quiet hours, avoid unsafe flames, secure pets, and communicate
about concerns when safe. The placard and QR sticker point to this standard. Do not put a name, phone,
recovery code, medical information, or exact location on them.

## Private-property permission

A business/property representative may document a specific area, dates, conditions, access to agreed
facilities, and copies retained by both sides. The signer must have authority; the form does not override
zoning, fire, health, accessibility, employment, insurance, landlord, or other legal requirements.

## Mutual-aid agreement

Groups may write voluntary commitments for supplies, waste/sharps, quiet hours, privacy, and conflict
pauses. No one must join, disclose a diagnosis, surrender belongings, or accept unsafe mediation. Threats,
violence, fire, weapons, exploitation, and medical emergencies require safety and appropriate emergency or
outreach response.

## Print safely

Keep health cards and document trackers private. Verify service hours and rules before travel. Print in
large type when possible, offer translations and read-aloud support, and provide an accessible digital
alternative.
