# Community guides

These guides are practical, voluntary, and non-punitive. They do not create a right to camp, promise a
service, or replace local law, medical advice, emergency response, or legal aid.

## For the public

People living outdoors are neighbors. Frustration about blocked paths, noise, or waste is real, but it
does not justify harassment, filming, taking belongings, or posting a person’s location. Ask before
approaching, offer a specific choice, accept “no,” and use outreach or 2-1-1 rather than confrontation.
Never photograph or publish a person, tent, medical detail, or exact location without consent.

## For people living outdoors

You do not owe a stranger your full story to deserve help. Ask what will be public, decline optional
fields, request a correction, and keep recovery cards private. Choose safer locations where possible;
keep exits and accessible paths clear; reduce noise at agreed quiet hours; and ask outreach where toilets,
showers, water, trash, sharps disposal, charging, storage, and medical care are available.

## Living outdoors with respect

- Avoid doorways, curb ramps, fire lanes, transit stops, unstable slopes, flood channels, rail corridors,
  and active construction.
- Bag trash; never burn it. Put sharps in a rigid closed container and ask a service site for disposal.
- Keep food sealed, wash hands, protect wounds, and seek help for heat illness, infection, overdose, or
  other urgent symptoms.
- Keep music and visitors controlled, secure pets, clean up after them, and leave a clear path out.
- Do not camp on private property without permission; permission forms do not override law or safety rules.

## Voluntary neighbor materials

The [Respectful Neighbor placard](https://github.com/writerslogic/onrecord/blob/main/docs/respectful-neighbor-tent-placard.md)
and QR sticker are signals of voluntary cleanliness, access, quiet, safety, and communication commitments.
They are not leases, legal defenses, or enforcement exemptions.

## Practical print resources

The repository also includes a private emergency health card, document-recovery tracker, mutual-aid
mediation agreement, and San Diego verify-before-travel resource directory. Keep health/recovery details
off public maps and confirm hours, eligibility, and capacity before traveling.
