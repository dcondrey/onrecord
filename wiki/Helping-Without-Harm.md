# Helping without harm

People often ask how to help someone who is homeless. The answer is not always money, a tent, or a bed.
Sometimes it is a respectful conversation, a service referral, help replacing a document, or treating
someone as a person whose “no” matters.

## Enabling is not a synonym for kindness

Support can become unsafe when it repeatedly removes accountability for serious harm, funds exploitation,
creates dependency without choice, or requires the helper to ignore danger. But a meal, ride, gift, or
place to sleep is not automatically enabling. Do not use the word to shame someone for surviving or
accepting help. Ask what they want and what can continue safely without coercion.

## Perpetuating harm

Good intentions can perpetuate harm when they demand a traumatic story, treat someone as a publicity
project, give unsuitable items without asking, make promises that cannot be kept, move someone without a
safe destination, or publish a name, face, camp, health detail, or recovery code “to get help.” Repair
starts with listening, permission, correction, and changed practice.

## True help

True help preserves agency and safety:

- Ask what would be useful and accept “no.”
- Offer a practical choice without demanding a story, payment, or gratitude.
- Share 2-1-1, day-center, medical, hygiene, food, document, or legal-aid information.
- Help with a call, form, ID, appointment, charging, or transportation only if invited.
- Respect privacy, belongings, pets, pronouns, language, quiet, and boundaries.
- Keep promises small and honest; follow up only with permission.
- Keep your own limits: you can leave, say no, ask for backup, and avoid unsafe situations.

Respect is not a consolation prize until a bed appears. Dignity, truthful information, consent, and a
safe relationship are real help. For immediate danger use emergency services; for current San Diego
referrals start with 2-1-1. On Record is not a live shelter-placement system.
